svh, dvh and lvh: Choose a Mobile Viewport Height

Choose a viewport-height unit for mobile sections. Compare stable small viewports with dynamic sizing and test browser bars, long content and keyboards.

By Penta AIPublished 27 October 20262 min read

In this guide

  1. Make a landing section a minimum, not a ceiling
  2. Separate three sizing goals
  3. Test the events that desktop resizing misses

For a mobile introduction that should fit while browser controls are visible, start with a minimum height based on svh. For a surface that should resize as those controls expand or retract, consider dvh. Use lvh only when sizing against the largest viewport is intentional. The right unit depends on whether stability or filling the changing visible area matters more.

Make a landing section a minimum, not a ceiling

.intro {
  box-sizing: border-box;
  min-block-size: 100vh;
  min-block-size: 100svh;
  padding: 2rem 1.25rem;
  display: grid;
  align-content: center;
}

In a normal horizontal writing mode, this gives the section a viewport-height minimum while allowing long content to make it taller. The earlier vh declaration is a fallback. Do not turn it into a fixed height plus hidden overflow: enlarged text and additional copy must still have somewhere to go.

Reference: MDN: viewport-relative length units.

Separate three sizing goals

  • svh uses the small viewport and stays stable as browser controls retract.
  • lvh uses the large viewport; browser controls may cover content when expanded.
  • dvh follows the dynamic viewport and can change the layout during scrolling.

For a project preview, a stable introduction may be preferable to an area that grows and shrinks around its headline. For an application shell, dynamic sizing may be useful, but its scroll regions and focus behavior need a separate review. The unit by itself does not design those interactions.

Test the events that desktop resizing misses

On a real mobile browser, load the page with controls expanded, scroll until they retract, then reverse direction. Rotate the device and zoom text where supported. Focus a field near the bottom and open the on-screen keyboard. Keyboard resizing policies differ, so do not assume dvh guarantees that a focused field or submit button remains visible.

Use a tall sample paragraph and the longest expected heading. Record whether the section grows naturally, whether any nested scroll area becomes difficult to reach, and whether the page jumps during browser-bar transitions. A desktop device frame is useful for width checks but cannot establish all of this behavior.

If a sticky header shares the screen, decide whether the section includes the header or sits below it. Subtract a header height only when that height is actually controlled; a wrapped navigation bar can invalidate a hardcoded subtraction.
