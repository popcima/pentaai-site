Give an Illustration Context with figure and figcaption

Group an illustration with a meaningful caption while keeping image alternatives useful. Explain the takeaway in readable text without duplicating every sentence.

By Penta AIPublished 19 November 20262 min read

In this guide

  1. Write the relationship before styling it
  2. Avoid duplicate or misleading descriptions
  3. Check the result in the reading flow

Figure groups self-contained content with an optional caption. Use figcaption to explain what the reader should take from an illustration, while the image’s alt text provides an appropriate alternative to the visual. A caption and an alternative can serve different purposes; one does not automatically replace the other.

Write the relationship before styling it

<figure class="comparison">
  <img src="/images/example-comparison.webp" width="1200" height="630"
       alt="Two cards show the same label wrapped into one line and two lines.">
  <figcaption>
    Example: allow the card to grow when its label wraps instead of fixing its height.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

Replace the specimen image with an actual matching illustration. The alternative describes the relevant visual comparison; the caption states its design implication. If the visual contains critical numbers or instructions, provide those in ordinary surrounding text too, so they remain easy to read, select and search.

Reference: MDN figure.

Reference: MDN figcaption.

Avoid duplicate or misleading descriptions

Do not repeat a long caption word for word in alt merely because both fields are available. Decide what information the image contributes and what the caption adds. A purely decorative image can have an empty alternative when the surrounding content already provides everything needed, but that decision must follow the image’s actual purpose.

Use one figcaption as the first or last child of its figure. A visually nearby paragraph outside the figure is not the same structural relationship. Multiple images can belong to one figure when they form a single comparison; unrelated screenshots often need separate context.

Check the result in the reading flow

  • Read the page with images unavailable.
  • Review the alternative and caption together for repetition.
  • Check the illustration and caption at narrow widths and enlarged text.
  • Confirm any credit or source attribution is accurate and linked where appropriate.

A caption should identify an example as an example, not imply a product test that never occurred. Use the UI-image alternative guide for visual descriptions and responsive image setup for loading and sizing. Keep the figure useful even when its polished visual treatment is not available.
