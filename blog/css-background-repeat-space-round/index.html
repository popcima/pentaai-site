Choose Gaps or Resizing for Repeated Background Tiles

Compare background-repeat space and round using a fixed-size tile, then inspect narrow widths where tile count and spacing change.

By Penta AIPublished 20 November 20261 min read

In this guide

  1. Compare one axis at a time
  2. Expect changes at tile-count boundaries
  3. Resize around the difficult widths

Use background-repeat: space when complete tiles should keep their size and gaps can absorb the remaining room. Use round when tiles may resize to fit without gaps. This decision is different from stretching one large image with cover.

Compare one axis at a time

.tile-strip {
  inline-size: 310px;
  block-size: 60px;
  background-image: linear-gradient(#b7d5ff, #b7d5ff);
  background-size: 90px 40px;
  background-position: left center;
  background-repeat: space no-repeat;
}

The solid gradient is a self-contained tile specimen. With no padding or border, a 310px-wide strip fits three 90px tiles and distributes the remaining 40px into two 20px gaps. Switch only space to round to compare a gapless fit that adjusts tile width. Use a visibly asymmetric real motif next to reveal distortion.

Reference: MDN background-repeat.

Expect changes at tile-count boundaries

With space, the first and last tiles normally meet the edges when multiple tiles fit. If only one fits, background-position becomes relevant; if even one cannot fit, clipping can occur. Round adjusts the repetition to the available dimension. Do not infer the final pattern from the nominal background-size alone.

The first repeat keyword controls the horizontal direction and the second the vertical direction. Multiple background layers need corresponding comma-separated settings; inspect the layer you actually intend to change.

Resize around the difficult widths

Check widths just above and below the point where another tile fits. Look for abrupt spacing changes, clipped logos or squashed decorative marks. A decorative texture can tolerate adjustments that a recognizable brand mark cannot. Keep meaningful labels as content instead of embedding them in a repeated background.

Review background sizing and position calculations when the tiling is correct but its scale or placement is not.
