Choose Alpha or Luminance for an SVG Mask

Compare opaque black masks under alpha and luminance modes, inspect mask-type on the definition, and distinguish transparency from brightness.

By Penta AIPublished 24 November 20261 min read

In this guide

  1. Compare identical black mask content
  2. Inspect the mask definition and the consuming style
  3. Test simple colors before complex artwork

An SVG mask can use transparency or luminance to determine how much of a graphic remains visible. Opaque black is the useful diagnostic: it reveals content in an alpha mask, but hides it in a luminance mask. The same painted mask can therefore produce opposite-looking results.

Compare identical black mask content

<svg viewBox="0 0 240 100" width="360" height="150" aria-hidden="true">
  <defs>
    <mask id="review-alpha" style="mask-type:alpha">
      <rect width="100%" height="100%" fill="black"/>
    </mask>
    <mask id="review-luma" style="mask-type:luminance">
      <rect width="100%" height="100%" fill="black"/>
    </mask>
  </defs>
  <rect x="10" y="10" width="90" height="80" fill="#1756a0" mask="url(#review-alpha)"/>
  <rect x="140" y="10" width="90" height="80" fill="#1756a0" mask="url(#review-luma)"/>
</svg>

The left rectangle remains visible through opaque mask alpha; the right is hidden by the black luminance mask. Use unique IDs when repeating the specimen. A checkerboard or border around the SVG can help distinguish hidden content from a loading failure.

Reference: MDN SVG mask type alpha luminance.

Inspect the mask definition and the consuming style

Mask-type applies to the SVG mask element. It is not a general switch for every PNG or gradient mask. A consuming mask-mode declaration can override the mode, so inspect both sides when the definition appears correct but the result differs.

In a luminance mask, brightness contributes to visibility along with alpha. In an alpha mask, changing opaque black to opaque white does not change its alpha coverage. That comparison isolates a mode mismatch from a genuinely transparent source.

Test simple colors before complex artwork

Try opaque black, opaque white and a partially transparent shape before introducing a detailed mask. Check the mask’s coordinate system, bounds and reference ID if both modes unexpectedly hide everything.

Keep essential information available independently of a decorative masked treatment. When exporting or handing off the asset, include the mask mode and the source content, not just a screenshot of the final cutout.
