Show Inserted and Deleted Copy in a Design Review

Use ins and del for edits, provide a plain-language change summary, and distinguish revision markup from text that is simply no longer relevant.

By Penta AIPublished 21 November 20261 min read

In this guide

  1. Mark the smallest useful replacement
  2. Keep dates and source context truthful
  3. Review the comparison and the final copy

Ins and del describe inserted and deleted content. They can make a copy review easier to compare, but a colored underline or strike-through alone is not a dependable explanation of the change. Pair revision markup with a short visible summary.

Mark the smallest useful replacement

<p>Change summary: the action label now explains what is exported.</p>
<p>
  Button label:
  <del>Continue</del>
  <ins>Export review</ins>
</p>

The wording is an illustrative design edit, not a statement about a product release. Keeping each element around only the changed phrase makes the comparison easier to scan. The ordinary summary remains useful if a reader does not perceive or hear the revision styling.

Reference: MDN ins.

Keep dates and source context truthful

The datetime attribute can record when an edit occurred, and cite can identify a resource explaining it. Neither automatically creates a visible date or clickable explanation for readers. If that information matters, include it in the surrounding content and use real dates and accessible source links.

Use revision elements for an actual edit history. Text that is merely no longer accurate or relevant can have a different semantic treatment, such as s. Do not imply a deletion-and-insertion event just to get a preferred visual effect.

Review the comparison and the final copy

Check that the old and new phrases remain distinguishable without color. Listen to the paragraph with the assistive technology your team supports; insertion and deletion boundaries may need an explicit visible explanation. Then read the final sentence with deleted text removed to ensure the proposed copy stands on its own.

In a handoff, distinguish proposed changes from accepted ones and link to the decision when available. Use machine-readable dates for real timestamps and the handoff checklist to capture ownership and acceptance criteria.
