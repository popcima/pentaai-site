Review Tooltips for Hover, Focus and Dismissal

Test whether temporary help can be reached, read and dismissed without losing context. Distinguish a brief tooltip from interactive content that needs another pattern.

By Penta AIPublished 16 November 20262 min read

In this guide

  1. Write a reproducible interaction sequence
  2. Keep the additional content operable and predictable
  3. Stress the placement and timing

Review a tooltip as a temporary interaction, not just a small floating label. A useful check asks how it opens, whether the pointer can reach it, how long it stays available and how it can be dismissed. Keyboard users need a way to discover the same supporting information.

Write a reproducible interaction sequence

1. Tab to the help trigger.
2. Read the additional explanation.
3. Dismiss it without moving focus.
4. Open it with the pointer.
5. Move the pointer from the trigger onto the help.
6. Leave the relevant area and verify the closing behavior.

Use an actual label from the interface, such as “Export quality,” and record what should happen at each step. A forced hover state in DevTools can help inspect colors but cannot prove that the pointer can cross a gap without closing the content.

Reference: W3C guidance on content appearing on hover or focus.

Keep the additional content operable and predictable

For author-created hover/focus content, review dismissibility, hoverability and persistence under the criterion’s conditions and exceptions. Escape is a common dismissal mechanism. Dismissal should not require a user to move focus away merely to uncover something beneath the tooltip.

Do not hide essential instructions only in a tooltip. A user may never trigger it, and its short-lived format is a poor home for long explanations. If the content contains links, buttons or a task to complete, choose an appropriate interactive popover or dialog instead of styling it as a tooltip and ignoring focus behavior.

The HTML title attribute is not a complete custom-tooltip implementation. Browser-managed title presentation and timing are not controlled in the same way, and it should not be the only naming or instruction strategy for a control.

Stress the placement and timing

  • Test keyboard focus and pointer hover independently.
  • Move slowly across the boundary between trigger and content.
  • Dismiss while leaving the pointer and focus in place.
  • Check viewport edges, scrolling ancestors and enlarged text.
  • Try touch input and confirm essential help remains available.

Record the closing rules and whether dismissal remains respected until the next intentional trigger. Reappearing immediately after Escape can make a technically present dismissal key ineffective in practice. Use the help-panel workflow when the information needs a deliberate open action or richer interaction.
